# Writing Guidelines (MC-02-03 §2)

## Abbreviations (§2.1)
Avoid informal spellings/abbreviations — always use the full correct spelling.

| Informal | Correct |
|---|---|
| & | and |
| 1st | first |
| HW | hardware |
| hi-(noun) | high-(noun) |
| I/F | interface |
| info | information |
| next-gen | next-generation |
| nite | night |
| SW | software |
| 3rd | third |
| tech | technology |
| thru | through |
| w/ | with |

## Commonly Misused Words (§2.2)
- **A vs. An:** "an" before a word that *sounds* like it starts with a vowel ("an MCU," "a microcontroller").
- **Affect vs. Effect:** affect = verb (influence); effect = noun (result).
- **i.e. vs. e.g.:** both are confusing to readers — avoid using either; write "that is" or "for example" instead.
- **etc.:** avoid — it's ambiguous. Don't use "etc." or "and more" at the end of a list; instead name the
  category, e.g., "...aviation, defense, space and other challenging applications."
- **Than vs. Then:** than = comparison; then = condition/consequence.

## Innuendos and Double Entendres (§2.3)
Never use innuendos or double entendres — indirect or suggestive alternate meanings are offensive
and reflect poorly on Microchip. Flag any phrasing with an unintended second meaning.

## Latin Words (§2.4)
Never pluralize a Latin word by just adding "s." Match verb case to singular/plural form correctly.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| Addendum | Addenda |
| Antenna | Antennae |
| Criterion | Criteria |
| Datum | Data |
| Erratum | Errata (never "Erratas") |
| Index | Indices or Indexes |
| Parenthesis | Parentheses |

## References to Third-Party Companies and Products (§2.5)
References to third parties are informational only, not an endorsement.

**Non-competitors:**
- Avoid mentioning other companies unless essential; use official company names.
- Avoid specific product/part numbers except in Bills of Materials (BOMs) — numbers can go stale.
- Prefer generic product descriptions over specific brand names/trademarks where possible.
- Don't say how/where to obtain a non-competitive product.
- Use correct trademark symbols on company/product names.

**Competitors:**
- Stay factually accurate — technical specs, benchmarks, and claims must be verifiable and backed by
  reliable data. Cite independent third-party sources when possible. Do NOT use analyst/industry data
  unless Microchip purchased the report or has written permission.
- Avoid subjective/emotional language ("far superior") — use objective, measurable comparisons instead
  ("20% higher data transfer rate, per test results published in Y").
- Respect competitor trademarks/brand names — proper capitalization, correct ™/® use, and clearly
  state no affiliation/endorsement.
- Frame comparisons around customer benefit, not attacking the competitor.
- Stay within legal boundaries (e.g., FTC truthful-advertising requirements); avoid anything that
  could read as defamatory.
- Acknowledge competitor advantages fairly where they exist; present alternatives as options, not attacks
  (e.g., "Comparable Products," "Alternative Solutions").
- Provide balanced comparisons (strengths and areas for improvement on both sides).
- Never reference confidential/proprietary competitor information (NDA pricing, unreleased roadmaps).
- Include disclaimers when comparisons depend on specific test/environment conditions.

## Sentence Fragments (§2.6)
A sentence fragment (missing subject or verb) never gets a period, and should only appear in bulleted
text or headlines/titles — never in regular paragraphs.

## Subject/Verb Agreement (§2.7)
- Singular subject → singular verb; plural subject → plural verb, even with an intervening adjective
  phrase (verb agrees with the true subject, not the nearest noun).
- Subjects joined by "and" → plural verb. Subjects joined by "or": verb agrees with the closer subject
  (or the last one, if mixed singular/plural).
- Watch for words that sound plural but are singular: family, series, group, category, team, portfolio,
  range — these all take a singular verb ("This family of MCUs is versatile").

## Deprecated Terminology — Master/Slave, etc. (§2.8)
- Use "Microchip Preferred" replacement terminology (per Applications Council guidance) instead of
  "master," "slave," and similar deprecated terms.
- Because correct replacement is context-dependent, flag the term for the writer/SME to resolve rather
  than silently substituting — the correct wording depends on the specific technical use case.
- If a writer determines the deprecated term must stay for technical accuracy, that requires
  justification to the Marcom team.

## Use of "Microchip" and "MCHP" (§2.9)
- No comma before "Inc." in "Microchip Technology Inc."
- "Microchip" in typed text is NOT registered — never show it with ® (only the logo artwork is
  registered). Never substitute the stylized logo wordmark for typed "Microchip."
- Never shorten to "MCHP" except when specifically referring to the stock ticker symbol. Never use
  MCHP in a product/solution name or a URL.
- Never use "Microchip" as a word inside a coined acronym (e.g., avoid inventing "MEG" from
  "Microchip Editing Guidelines").

## Voice (§2.10)
- Default voice: conversational, customer-focused — refer to Microchip as "we/us/our," the reader as
  "you/your." Still professional, never overly casual, always aligned with Microchip's Guiding Values.
- **This conversational voice does NOT apply to technical documentation or legal content** — those
  stay formal/impersonal per their own conventions.
- Review questions to ask: Is it well written? Customer-focused? Culturally appropriate for a global
  audience? Free of anything offensive/disrespectful/rude?

## Words to Avoid (§2.11)
Flag these — they imply an unsupportable promise or absolute claim. If used, the writer should be able
to provide evidence:

**Absolute/promissory (avoid or scrutinize heavily):**
assure/assurance, ensure, faultless, guarantee/guaranteed, ideal, optimal, perfect, promise, risk free,
ultimate, unique, zero risk

**Superlative/claim words (use judiciously, must be evidence-backed):**
all, always, best, best-selling, best-value, biggest, eliminates, every, fastest, first, greatest, highest,
highest-rated, leading, longest, maximum, most, most customers, never, number one, oldest, smallest,
strongest
